## 1. Framework Overview

### What is REACH?

REACH is a 5-stage financial journey framework that guides users from financial confusion to financial freedom. Every feature, page, recommendation, and interaction in Monitrax maps to one of the five REACH stages.

```
R E A C H  Financial Freedom

R — Reveal      "See your full picture"
E — Establish   "Build your foundation"
A — Act         "Take control of your money"
C — Compound    "Grow and compound your wealth"
H — Harvest     "Reap your financial freedom"
```

### Why REACH Exists

Most people are financially stressed. Research shows:

- 72% of people feel stressed about money (APA, 2022)
- 33% of Australians report high financial stress (NAB, 2023)
- 29% of Australian households cannot raise $2,000 for an emergency (ABS, 2020)
- Financial stress reduces cognitive function by 13 IQ points (Mani et al., 2013, Science)
- Financial literacy alone explains only 0.1% of variance in financial behaviour (Fernandes et al., 2014)

People don't need another spreadsheet. They need a guide. REACH is that guide.

### The Core Belief

> **Success = Accumulating wealth + Reducing debt.**
>
> Every feature in Monitrax serves one of these two goals, organised into a journey that transforms an overwhelmed, avoidant user into a confident, thriving wealth builder.

### The Emotional Arc

```
😰 "I'm lost"  →  😟 "I can see it"  →  😤 "I'm taking charge"  →  😊 "I'm building"  →  😎 "I'm free"
     REVEAL          ESTABLISH              ACT                    COMPOUND            HARVEST
```

## 2. The Five Stages

### Stage 1: REVEAL

**"See your full picture"**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it means** | Face your full financial reality — no judgment, just clarity. See every account, every debt, every dollar in and out. End the avoidance. |
| **Key question** | "What do I actually have, owe, earn, and spend?" |
| **Emotional shift** | From **avoidance** to **awareness** |
| **Milestone** | "I can see my complete financial picture in one place." |
| **Measurable** | All accounts connected via Basiq. Net worth calculated. Income and spending visible. |
| **Monitrax section** | My Accounts (Accounts, Loans, Income, Spending, Transactions, Recurring) |

**Why it comes first:** Every proven financial framework starts with awareness. You cannot change what you cannot see. Financial avoidance — not opening bills, not checking balances, not facing debts — is the #1 predictor of low net worth (Klontz, 2011). Simply SEEING your finances reduces anxiety because avoidance amplifies fear of the unknown.

---

### Stage 2: ESTABLISH

**"Build your foundation"**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it means** | Create a safety net. Cover your basics. Build an emergency buffer so one unexpected bill doesn't send you backward. |
| **Key question** | "Can I handle a financial shock without going into debt?" |
| **Emotional shift** | From **fear** to **stability** |
| **Milestone** | "I have 3 months of expenses saved. My bills are covered. I can sleep at night." |
| **Measurable** | Emergency fund reaches 3 months of expenses. Bills paid on time. No new consumer debt. |
| **Monitrax section** | Tracked through Financial Health score + CFO recommendations (not a dedicated sidebar section) |

**Why this stage matters:** Maslow's hierarchy applied to finance — you cannot focus on wealth building when survival is threatened. 29% of Australian households can't raise $2,000 for an emergency. An emergency fund provides the psychological safety that enables all future stages. This maps directly to the Barefoot Investor's "Mojo" bucket.

---

### Stage 3: ACT

**"Take control of your money"**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it means** | Take the reins. Know where every dollar goes. Set a budget and stick to it. Attack debt strategically. Optimise your tax position. |
| **Key question** | "Am I spending less than I earn, and am I actively reducing my debt?" |
| **Emotional shift** | From **overwhelm** to **empowerment** |
| **Milestone** | "I spend less than I earn. I have a debt paydown plan. I'm in the driver's seat." |
| **Measurable** | Positive monthly cashflow. Debt decreasing month-on-month. Budget adherence above 80%. |
| **Monitrax section** | My Budget (Budget, Cashflow, Debt Freedom, Tax) |

**Why ACT is the turning point:** This is where debt reduction happens — half of the success equation. The Debt Snowball method (paying smallest debt first) is proven to be more effective than mathematically optimal approaches because behavioural momentum matters more than interest rate optimisation (Northwestern Kellogg, 2012). Self-efficacy — your belief that you CAN manage money — is a stronger predictor of financial outcomes than financial knowledge (Farrell, Fry & Risse, 2016, Australian study).

---

### Stage 4: COMPOUND

**"Grow and compound your wealth"**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it means** | Now that you're stable and in control, start accumulating. Buy property. Invest in shares and super. Build assets. Grow your net worth deliberately. |
| **Key question** | "Is my net worth growing? Am I building something that lasts?" |
| **Emotional shift** | From **surviving** to **building** |
| **Milestone** | "My net worth is growing. I own property. I'm investing regularly." |
| **Measurable** | Net worth increasing quarter-on-quarter. Investment portfolio growing. Property equity building. |
| **Monitrax section** | My Wealth (Properties, Investments, Assets) |

**Why COMPOUND:** Einstein reportedly called compound interest "the eighth wonder of the world." This stage is where wealth accumulation happens — the other half of the success equation. The Endowment Effect means once people see their wealth listed out, they value it more and protect it more carefully. The Goal-Gradient Effect means the closer you get to a wealth target, the harder you work.

---

### Stage 5: HARVEST

**"Reap your financial freedom"**

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it means** | Your wealth works for you. Passive income approaches or exceeds expenses. You make financial decisions from abundance, not scarcity. |
| **Key question** | "Can I live the life I want without financial worry?" |
| **Emotional shift** | From **building** to **freedom** |
| **Milestone** | "My passive income covers my expenses. I make choices from abundance." |
| **Measurable** | Financial Health score consistently high. Passive income growing. Debt minimal or zero. |
| **Monitrax section** | My CFO (Health, Actions, Progress) |

**Why HARVEST:** This directly connects to the Barefoot Investor's Phase 3 (HARVEST). Vicki Robin's "Crossover Point" (Your Money or Your Life) defines it precisely: the moment when investment income exceeds expenses. At this stage, the CFO shifts from triage to optimisation. The identity transformation is complete — from "someone struggling with money" to "someone who has mastered money."

## 3. Evidence Base & Psychology

### REACH Maps to Every Proven Framework

| REACH | Barefoot Investor | Ramsey Baby Steps | Your Money or Your Life | Maslow Financial | CFPB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reveal** | Step 1: Date night | — | Step 1: Lifetime earnings | Level 1: Cash flow | — |
| **Establish** | Step 2: Buckets + Mojo | Steps 1+3: Emergency fund | — | Level 2: Safety | Element 2: Absorb shocks |
| **Act** | Step 3: Domino debts | Step 2: Debt snowball | Steps 3-6: Track + minimise | Level 2-3 | Element 1: Day-to-day control |
| **Compound** | Steps 4-5: Home + Super | Steps 4-5: Invest + education | Steps 7-8: Maximise + invest | Level 3: Accumulation | Element 3: On track |
| **Harvest** | Steps 7-9: Mortgage-free + legacy | Steps 6-7: Pay off home + give | Step 9: Crossover Point | Level 4-5: Freedom + Legacy | Element 4: Freedom |

### Barefoot Investor Alignment (Australian)

The Barefoot Investor (Scott Pape, 2M+ copies in Australia) uses 3 phases with 9 steps:

```
BAREFOOT:     PLANT              →        GROW           →      HARVEST
              ─────                       ─────                  ───────
REACH:        REVEAL → ESTABLISH → ACT    → COMPOUND      →      HARVEST
```

Barefoot's broad PLANT phase maps to REACH stages R, E, and A — broken out because overwhelmed users need more granular guidance through the critical early phase.

### Key Research Backing Each Stage

**REVEAL — Awareness works:**
- Klontz et al. (2011): Financial avoidance is the money script most correlated with low income/net worth
- Prochaska's TTM: Moving from Precontemplation to Contemplation is the critical first step
- CFP Board Step 1: "Understanding the client's circumstances" — every planner starts here

**ESTABLISH — Safety enables growth:**
- Maslow (1943): Safety needs must be met before growth needs
- Barefoot's Mojo: $2,000 → 3 months emergency fund
- CFPB Element 2: "Capacity to absorb a financial shock"
- ABS (2020): 29% of Australian households can't raise $2,000

**ACT — Self-efficacy through mastery:**
- Bandura (1977): Mastery experiences are the #1 source of self-efficacy
- Northwestern Kellogg (2012): Debt snowball (smallest first) is more effective behaviourally
- Farrell, Fry & Risse (2016, Australian): Financial self-efficacy predicts outcomes better than literacy
- Fernandes et al. (2014): Financial education alone = 0.1% variance. Action changes behaviour.

**COMPOUND — Growth through accumulation:**
- Barefoot Steps 4-5: Buy home + super to 15%
- Ramsey Steps 4-5: Invest 15% + education savings
- Goal-Gradient Effect: Motivation increases as you approach targets

**HARVEST — Freedom as the outcome:**
- Robin's Crossover Point: Investment income > expenses = financial independence
- Barefoot Phase 3: HARVEST — mortgage-free, retirement secured, legacy planned
- Maslow Levels 4-5: Freedom and self-actualisation

### Psychological Principles Embedded in REACH

| Principle | Source | How REACH applies it |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-efficacy through small wins** | Bandura (1977) | Each stage has a clear, achievable milestone |
| **Stage-matched interventions** | Prochaska & DiClemente (1983) | CFO adapts recommendations to user's REACH stage |
| **Automation over willpower** | Thaler & Benartzi (2004) | Basiq auto-populates; buckets auto-categorise |
| **Financial hierarchy of needs** | Newcomb (2016), Maslow (1943) | REACH stages follow Maslow's financial hierarchy |
| **Behavioural momentum** | Ramsey, Barefoot | Debt snowball in ACT stage; small debts first |
| **Progressive disclosure** | NN/Group | Sidebar shows journey stages, not overwhelming feature lists |
| **Non-judgmental framing** | Financial Therapy Association | "Reveal" not "audit"; "Debt Freedom" not "Debt Planner" |
| **Warm language** | SDT: Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness | "My Accounts", "My Wealth", "My CFO" — ownership language |

### The Intervention That Actually Works

The research is clear: **Financial literacy alone does not change behaviour.** (Fernandes et al., 2014, meta-analysis of 168 studies). What DOES work:

1. **Self-efficacy** — Belief you can manage money (Lown, 2011)
2. **Just-in-time guidance** — Advice at the point of decision, not in advance (Kaiser et al., 2022)
3. **Automation** — Make the right behaviour the default (Thaler & Benartzi, 2004)
4. **Simplification** — Fewer choices = more action (Beshears et al., 2013)
5. **Social proof** — "Most people in your situation..." (Hallsworth et al., 2017)

REACH builds all five into Monitrax's design.

```
👨‍👩‍👧‍👦  My Household                  — Who you're doing this for

                                    Transactions | Recurring

🏘️  My Wealth      [COMPOUND]    — Properties | Investments | Assets

🧭  My CFO         [HARVEST]     — Health | Actions | Progress

📁  Reports                      — Reports | Documents
⚙️  Settings                     — Profile | Security | Household | Billing
```

### Why 8 Items

- **Miller's Law (7 plus or minus 2):** 8 items is within cognitive limits
- **No scrolling:** Fits on any screen without scrollbar
- **No accordions:** Every item is one click away — flat list, zero expand/collapse friction
- **Competitor benchmark:** Monarch Money (10), Copilot (8), Simplifi (7), YNAB (3)
- **Every feature preserved:** 19 pages accessible via tabs within 8 sidebar items

### The Journey Story

```
HOME           → "Welcome back. Here's where you stand."
MY HOUSEHOLD   → "Remember who you're doing this for."
MY ACCOUNTS    → "Here's your full picture."                    [REVEAL]
MY BUDGET      → "Here's how you're taking control."            [ACT]
MY WEALTH      → "Here's what you're building."                 [COMPOUND]
MY CFO         → "Here's your path to freedom."                 [HARVEST]
REPORTS        → "Here's the proof."
```

### Where ESTABLISH Lives

Stage 2 (ESTABLISH) does not have a dedicated sidebar section. It represents a **milestone state** — your emergency fund is built, your bills are current, you can handle a shock. Monitrax tracks this through:

- **Financial Health score:** Emergency fund component shows months of coverage
- **My CFO recommendations:** "Your emergency fund covers 1.2 months. Target: 3 months."
- **My Accounts:** Users can see their emergency savings in the Accounts tab
- **Home dashboard:** REACH stage indicator shows when ESTABLISH is achieved

This mirrors how the Barefoot Investor handles Mojo — it's a bank account within the bucket system, not a separate activity.

## 6. Barefoot Investor CFO Integration

### Overview

The Monitrax CFO engine integrates the Barefoot Investor methodology (Australia's most trusted personal finance framework, 2M+ copies) with AI-powered personalisation. Where Barefoot stops at general advice in a book, Monitrax's CFO uses the user's ACTUAL data to deliver personalised, stage-matched guidance.

**Positioning:** Monitrax is "Barefoot Investor brought to life" — the app that turns Pape's serviette sketch into a living, intelligent, always-on financial system.

### Barefoot Bucket System Mapping

When a user connects via Basiq, Monitrax can auto-classify accounts into Barefoot buckets:

| Barefoot Bucket | Purpose | % of Income | Monitrax Mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blow** (Daily) | Everyday spending + bills | 60% | Transaction accounts (auto-detected via Basiq) |
| **Splurge** | Guilt-free short-term spending | 10% | Savings accounts tagged "splurge" or discretionary |
| **Smile** | Long-term savings (holiday, car) | 10% | Goal-tagged savings accounts |
| **Fire Extinguisher** | Debt payoff → then savings priorities | 20% | Calculated: income minus (Blow + Splurge + Smile) |
| **Mojo** | Emergency fund (separate bank) | Target: 3 months | High-interest savings accounts at different institution |
| **Grow** | Super + investments + property | Overflow | Investment accounts + super + property equity |

### Stage-Matched CFO Recommendations

The CFO adapts its advice based on the user's REACH stage AND Barefoot principles:

**Stage R (REVEAL) — CFO says:**
- "Let's connect your bank and see your full picture."
- "I've found 4 accounts, 2 loans, and 156 transactions. Here's your snapshot."
- "Your net worth is $X. Let's build from here."

**Stage E (ESTABLISH) — CFO says:**
- "Your emergency fund covers 0.8 months. Barefoot recommends 3 months in a separate Mojo account."
- "I've detected $340/month in subscriptions you may not need."
- "Set up automatic transfers: 60% Blow, 10% Splurge, 10% Smile, 20% Fire Extinguisher."

**Stage A (ACT) — CFO says:**
- "You have 4 debts totalling $28,400. Barefoot's Domino method: attack the $1,200 Afterpay first."
- "Paying $200 extra on your smallest debt will clear it in 6 months and save $340 in interest."
- "Your budget is 82% on track. Your biggest overspend: dining out (+$180 over budget)."
- "Tax tip: Your investment property expenses are $4,200 in deductible costs you haven't claimed."

**Stage C (COMPOUND) — CFO says:**
- "Your property equity grew $12,000 this quarter. LVR improved from 78% to 74%."
- "Barefoot recommends super at 15%. You're at 11.5%. Salary sacrificing $200/month saves $1,800 in tax."
- "Your investment portfolio is up 8.2% YTD. Consider rebalancing — tech is 45% of holdings."

**Stage H (HARVEST) — CFO says:**
- "Your passive income covers 62% of expenses. Target: 100% (Crossover Point)."
- "If you maintain current trajectory, you'll reach financial independence in 7.3 years."
- "Your mortgage has 8 years remaining. Extra $500/month cuts it to 5.1 years, saving $47,000."

### AI Enhancement Layer

Where Barefoot provides general rules, Monitrax's CFO adds:

| Barefoot Says | CFO Enhances With |
|---|---|
| "Domino your debts smallest first" | "Based on YOUR debts, domino saves $2,100 vs avalanche saves $2,400. I recommend domino because you'll clear 2 debts in 3 months — momentum matters." |
| "Super to 15%" | "At your marginal tax rate (37%), salary sacrificing $X saves $Y in tax THIS year." |
| "Set up your buckets" | "I've analysed your accounts. Your CBA Everyday = Blow. Your ING Savings = Mojo. Your Vanguard = Grow." |
| "Get the banker off your back" | "Your offset account has $45,000 saving $2,700/year in interest. Increasing to $60,000 saves an additional $1,125." |

## 7. Design Principles

### Language Rules

All user-facing text in Monitrax must follow these principles:

| Rule | Example (Wrong) | Example (Right) |
|---|---|---|
| **Use "My" for ownership** | Portfolio | My Wealth |
| **Use human words** | Expenses, Transactions | Spending, Activity |
| **Aspiration over diagnosis** | Debt Planner | Debt Freedom |
| **Simple over complex** | Budget Analysis | Budget |
| **Action over status** | Financial Health Report | How am I tracking? |
| **Progress over judgment** | You're at 45/100 | You've improved 5 points this month |

### Emotional Design Rules

1. **Lead with the positive** — Show what users HAVE before what they OWE
2. **Normalise struggle** — "72% of people feel stressed about money. You're not alone."
3. **Celebrate small wins** — "You paid off your Afterpay! That's one less debt."
4. **Never shame** — No red alerts for low balances. Use neutral tones for reality, warm tones for progress.
5. **Show the journey** — Every page should remind users which REACH stage they're in and what comes next

### Colour Psychology

| Usage | Colour | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Positive progress, wealth growth | Green/Teal | Growth, safety, "go" |
| Neutral information | Blue/Navy | Trust, calm, stability |
| Attention needed (not urgent) | Amber | Caution without alarm |
| Critical action required | Red | Reserved for genuine emergencies only |
| REACH stage indicator | Gradient from blue → green | Visual journey from start to freedom |

## 8. Stage Progression Logic

### How Monitrax Determines Your REACH Stage

Each user has a REACH stage calculated from measurable indicators:

| Stage | Criteria to Enter | Criteria to Complete |
|---|---|---|
| **Reveal** | User signs up | All accounts connected. Net worth visible. Income and spending tracked for 30+ days. |
| **Establish** | Reveal complete | Emergency fund >= 3 months expenses. Bills paid on time for 3+ months. No new consumer debt for 3+ months. |
| **Act** | Establish complete | Positive monthly cashflow for 3+ months. Total consumer debt decreasing. Budget set and adherence > 70%. |
| **Compound** | Act complete | Net worth increasing for 6+ months. Active investment contributions. Property/investment portfolio growing. |
| **Harvest** | Compound complete | Financial Health score > 75. Passive income > 50% of expenses. Minimal or zero consumer debt. |

### Stage Display

- **Home page:** Shows current REACH stage with progress bar toward next stage
- **Sidebar:** Subtle stage indicator next to the active section
- **My CFO:** Opens with "You're in Stage [X]: [Name]. Here's what to focus on."
- **Page headers:** Warm subtitles that reflect the stage context

### Users Can Be in Multiple Stages

Financial life is not perfectly linear. A user might:
- Be in COMPOUND (growing investments) while still working on ACT (paying off a car loan)
- Have completed ESTABLISH (emergency fund) but regress after an unexpected expense

The REACH stage represents the user's **primary focus**, not a rigid gate. The CFO acknowledges this: "You're primarily in COMPOUND, but I notice your emergency fund dropped below 2 months. Let's top that up."
