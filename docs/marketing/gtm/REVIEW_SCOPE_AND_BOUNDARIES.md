## 1. The legal premise in one paragraph

Australian financial services law (Corporations Act 2001, Chapter 7) distinguishes between **personal financial product advice** (regulated, requires an AFSL — Australian Financial Services Licence) and **general advice / information** (lower-bar, doesn't require AFSL but requires a "general advice warning" disclaimer). Monitrax does NOT hold an AFSL. Therefore: the Review CANNOT give personal financial product advice. It can give factual analysis of a person's data, general information about financial concepts, comparison to benchmarks, and identification of gaps — provided every statement stays on the *general* side of the line. The "reasonable person" test is the trap: even if Reza thinks he's giving general advice, if a reasonable person would expect Reza to have considered the customer's specific circumstances (and the Review literally has their data), the statement may be construed as personal advice. **The discipline is not "what did Reza intend" but "what would a reasonable person, reading this Review, conclude Reza was doing".**

## 2. What the Review IS (you CAN do this)

The Review is a **factual diagnostic + general-information document**. It is structured around the **TRAIL framework** (Track → Reduce → Anchor → Invest → Live) which is a non-product, non-personal-recommendation educational scaffold.

The Review may contain:

1. **Factual analysis of the customer's own data**
   - "Your net worth is AU$X based on the data you provided."
   - "Your debt-to-income ratio is X% (debt: $A; gross income: $B)."
   - "Your emergency fund covers X months of essential expenses."
   - "Your current savings rate is X% of gross income."

2. **Benchmark comparisons** (no product recommendation)
   - "The Australian Securities & Investments Commission's MoneySmart guide suggests 3–6 months of essential expenses as a common emergency-fund benchmark. Yours covers X months."
   - "Median household savings rate in Australia is approximately X% (ABS). Yours is X%."

3. **Gap identification** (factual, not prescriptive)
   - "You currently have no emergency fund."
   - "Your superannuation balance is below the median for your age cohort (median: $X; yours: $Y)."
   - "You have no will or power-of-attorney documents recorded."

4. **TRAIL stage observation** (educational framework, not personal recommendation)
   - "Based on the factors above, your financial profile aligns with the TRAIL **Reduce** stage. This stage is characterised by [generic stage description]."

5. **General information about financial concepts**
   - "An emergency fund is generally understood as 3–6 months of essential living expenses held in an accessible savings vehicle. It exists to absorb income disruption."
   - "Australian residents earning above the tax-free threshold pay marginal income tax at rates set by the ATO."

6. **Generic, class-level mentions of categories** (no specific product)
   - "Australians commonly use high-interest savings accounts for emergency funds, term deposits for medium-horizon savings, and superannuation for long-horizon retirement savings."
   - ❌ NOT: "You should use ING Savings Maximiser." ❌ NOT: "VAS ETF is appropriate for you."

7. **Questions for the customer to consider** (open-ended, not prescriptive)
   - "Have you considered whether your current emergency fund covers the income disruption scenarios most likely for your household?"
   - "Have you reviewed your superannuation fund's fees and performance against industry averages?"

8. **Pointer to licensed advice when appropriate**
   - "For personal recommendations on investment products tailored to your circumstances, consult a licensed financial adviser (AFSL holder)."
   - "For personal tax planning, consult a registered tax agent."

## 3. What the Review IS NOT (you CANNOT do these)

Every item below is an **automatic red flag** — if any of the following appears in a Review draft, it must be removed before delivery.

### 3.1 No specific product recommendations
- ❌ "Use ING Savings Maximiser for your emergency fund."
- ❌ "Switch to ART (Australian Retirement Trust) for your super."
- ❌ "Buy VAS ETF for Australian equities exposure."
- ❌ "Refinance your mortgage through loans.com.au."

**Even if the product is obviously the right call.** Even if every Australian financial podcast recommends it. The moment you name a specific product, you are giving personal advice.

### 3.2 No personal investment recommendations
- ❌ "You should invest in shares."
- ❌ "You should pay down your mortgage before investing."
- ❌ "Your allocation should be 70% equities, 30% bonds."
- ❌ "Sell your investment property and buy your principal residence."

### 3.3 No personal tax advice
- ❌ "You should salary-sacrifice $X to super to reduce your tax."
- ❌ "Claim the home-office deduction."
- ❌ "Restructure as a discretionary trust to minimise tax."

**Reason:** personal tax advice requires registration as a tax agent (separate from AFSL). Monitrax holds neither. General information about how Australian income tax works = fine. Anything that applies tax rules to the customer's specific circumstances = no.

### 3.4 No insurance recommendations
- ❌ "You need life insurance of $X."
- ❌ "Switch your TPD cover to your super fund."
- ❌ "You're under-insured by approximately $X."

### 3.5 No personal property / debt strategy recommendations
- ❌ "You should sell your investment property."
- ❌ "Refinance your home loan to a fixed rate."
- ❌ "Consolidate your credit cards into a personal loan."

### 3.6 No personal legal or estate advice
- ❌ "You should set up a discretionary trust."
- ❌ "You need a will. Use this template."
- ❌ "Your spouse should be the binding nomination on your super."

### 3.7 No predictive or comparative claims about products' future performance
- ❌ "ETF X is likely to outperform Y over the next decade."
- ❌ "Mortgage rates are expected to rise."

### 3.8 No language that implies the Review is personal advice
- ❌ "Based on your circumstances, we recommend..."
- ❌ "Our advice is..."
- ❌ "You should..."
- ❌ "Our personalised plan for you is..."

**Use these instead:**
- ✅ "Based on the data provided, your situation reflects..."
- ✅ "Things you might consider exploring with a licensed adviser..."
- ✅ "Many Australians at this stage choose to..."
- ✅ "A general-information observation is..."

## 4. DO say / DON'T say — the cheat sheet

For every claim in a Review, you should be able to map it to a "DO say" pattern. If a claim doesn't fit, drop it.

| Topic | ✅ DO say (general, factual) | ❌ DON'T say (personal advice) |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund | "Your emergency fund covers 0.7 months of essential expenses. The ASIC MoneySmart benchmark range is 3–6 months." | "You should put $5,000 into a high-interest savings account this month." |
| Savings rate | "Your savings rate is 4% of gross income. The Australian household median is approximately 8% (ABS 2024)." | "Increase your savings rate to 10% by cancelling your gym membership and Spotify." |
| Superannuation | "Your super balance is below the median for your age cohort. Consider reviewing your fund's fees and performance with a licensed adviser." | "Switch from AustralianSuper to ART and start salary-sacrificing $200/month." |
| Debt | "Your debt-to-income ratio is 4.8x. The APRA serviceability buffer is typically 7%." | "Refinance your home loan with [Bank X]. Pay down your credit card first." |
| Investment | "Asset classes Australians commonly use include cash, fixed income, Australian shares, international shares, listed property, and private assets." | "Buy VAS and IVV in a 60/40 split." |
| Property | "Your loan-to-value ratio on the investment property is 78%. The standard refinance LVR threshold is 80%." | "Sell the investment property and use the equity to pay down your principal residence." |
| Tax | "Australian residents earning above $X marginal tax rate Y%. Concessional super contributions are taxed at 15%." | "Salary-sacrifice $20,000 this year to reduce your taxable income to the next bracket." |
| Insurance | "You currently have no recorded life insurance. Industry guidance commonly references 10× annual income as a starting reference." | "You need $1.2M of life cover via [Insurer X]." |
| Estate | "You have no recorded will. The intestacy rules in your state are [link to NSW Trustee & Guardian]." | "Set up a testamentary trust and name your spouse as executor." |

**The pattern:** ✅ states a fact, references a benchmark, points at education. ❌ tells the customer what to do.

## 5. The disclaimer block — top + bottom of EVERY Review

The general advice warning is the legal safety net. It must appear **at the top AND bottom** of every delivered Review. Do not paraphrase. Do not abbreviate. Do not move it.

### 5.1 Top-of-Review disclaimer (must appear above any analysis)

```
IMPORTANT — GENERAL INFORMATION ONLY

This Financial Health Review is general information only.

It does NOT take into account your specific financial objectives, situation,
or needs, and it is NOT personal financial product advice.

Monitrax is not a licensed financial adviser, tax agent, or legal practitioner.
Monitrax does not hold an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL).

Before acting on any observation in this Review, consider whether it is
appropriate for your circumstances and, where relevant, consult a licensed
financial adviser, registered tax agent, or qualified legal practitioner.

The information here is current as at the date of delivery; financial
markets, tax law, and personal circumstances change.
```

### 5.2 Bottom-of-Review disclaimer (must appear below any analysis)

```
DISCLAIMER (full)

Monitrax provides this Financial Health Review as a general-information
diagnostic based on data the customer has provided. Monitrax is not
licensed to provide personal financial product advice, personal tax
advice, or legal advice.

No statement in this Review is a recommendation to acquire, dispose of,
hold, or vary any financial product. No statement here is personal advice
within the meaning of section 766B of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth).

Where products, asset classes, or strategies are mentioned, they are
mentioned for general information only. The mention of any product is
not an endorsement, recommendation, or solicitation.

For personal financial advice, contact an AFSL-licensed financial
adviser. For personal tax advice, contact a registered tax agent (TPB).
For legal questions, contact a qualified Australian legal practitioner.

Monitrax accepts no liability for any decision made by the customer in
reliance on this Review.

Customer signed acknowledgment: [yes/no, date]
Review delivered: [date]
Review prepared by: Reza Sadegh, Monitrax
```

The Review intake form (Step 3.3) must require the customer to tick:

## 7. Escalation — what to do when in doubt

The single biggest risk to Monitrax is one Review crossing the line and triggering an ASIC complaint. The discipline:

- **If you are not 100% sure a statement is on the general side of the line, REMOVE IT.** "If in doubt, leave it out." A Review with fewer observations + cleaner boundary is more defensible than a Review with more value + ambiguous language.
- **Default to factual framing.** "Your X is Y" instead of "You should change X to Z".
- **Default to "speak to a licensed adviser".** Cheap to add, removes the personal-advice characterisation from the statement.
- **When the customer asks a question that crosses the line** (in the discovery call, the intake form, or follow-up), the correct response is: *"That's a great question for a licensed financial adviser. The Review can show you the factual picture, but on whether you should specifically [X], you'd need someone with an AFSL to give you personal advice."*

When to ask a lawyer (and not ship the Review):
- The customer's situation involves a regulated structure you don't fully understand (SMSF, trust, complex international tax).
- The customer is asking for help with a product-switch decision (super fund switch, mortgage refinance, insurance change).
- The customer has indicated they will rely on the Review to make a transaction.
- You catch yourself wanting to write "you should" three times in one section.
