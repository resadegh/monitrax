### V2 — `annualGrossIncome` is fed the *net* total

`cashflow.annualGrossIncome` = **495,632.05**, which is exactly `income.annual.all.` **`netTotal`**. The actual `grossTotal` is **505,564.05**. A field named gross carries net. Same defect monthly: `cashflow.monthlyGrossIncome` 41,302.67 ≡ `income.monthly.all.netTotal` 41,302.67, while `grossTotal` is 42,130.34.

This also **explains MON-128**: Home's `$41,303/mo` against a declared gross of `$317,751/yr` is not a net-exceeds-gross paradox — it is a *different producer's net total*, mislabelled gross, rendered beside a third producer's annual figure.

### V3 — PAYG withholding has three values, and the rendered one is not the applied one

| Value | Producer | Rendered? |
|---|---|---|
| **$9,932.00/yr** | *unnamed* — the deduction actually applied: `income.annual.all.grossTotal 505,564.05 − netTotal 495,632.05` | no |
| **$11,128.70/yr** | `income.annual.all.paygWithholding` | **yes** — tax page `PAYG withheld $11,129` |
| **$36,197.69/yr** | `cashflow.annualPaygWithholding` | no |

**The identity `grossTotal − netTotal ≡ paygWithholding` fails by $1,196.70.** Confirmed against the type split: `byType.SALARY` gross 227,519.50 / net 217,587.50 is a difference of exactly 9,932.00, and OTHER and RENTAL carry gross ≡ net, so salary withholding is the entire deduction. The same shape holds monthly — 42,130.34 − 41,302.67 = **827.67** applied against a stated **927.39**, and `827.67 × 12 = 9,932.00`, `927.39 × 12 = 11,128.70`. Systematic, not rounding.

**The number the tax page shows Reza as withheld from him is not the number the engine withheld.**

And `annualNetIncome` = `495,632.05 − 36,197.69` — **PAYG subtracted a second time, from a figure that was already net, using the largest of the three.**
