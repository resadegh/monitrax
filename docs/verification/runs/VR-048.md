## The one finding — real, low, and correctly scoped

> `riskRadar` mints a fresh UUID and `detectedAt` on every capture, so two captures on identical code and
> data are never byte-identical.

**Verified in source before registering** (§19.2): `lib/cfo/riskRadar.ts:605` — `createRisk()` sets
`id: crypto.randomUUID()` and `detectedAt: new Date()` on every call, and risks are recomputed on every
`scanForRisks`. The Matrix's evidence: 19 leaves differed between its two captures, **all non-numeric** —
`masterFinancialService.calculatedAt` plus `risks.N.id` and `risks.N.detectedAt`. Zero numeric leaves
differed.

**It cannot affect a verdict**, because `diffBaselines` flattens to *numeric* leaves and these are
strings. Registered as **MON-158** (low): consumers today are the CFO advice chat and `aiAdvisor`, both
transient, and nothing persists a risk by id — so there is no dismissal state to lose right now. What it
means is that no consumer *can* key off a risk id, and a future "dismiss this risk" feature would break
silently on the first re-scan.

## The flake this nearly got blamed on — MON-159

Worth recording in full, because the wrong conclusion was reached twice before the right one.

Adding the ratchet, the **full** suite aborted with `exit 134` — a Prisma query-engine panic
(`query-engine-node-api/src/engine.rs:74`, *"thread caused non-unwinding panic"*). The obvious inference
was that the new test caused it, and two rounds of restructuring followed: first moving the 455 KB
`JSON.parse` out of vitest's collection phase, then caching it to a single parse. **Each looked like it
helped. Neither did** — the failure rate stayed around one run in three.

Running the suite **six times with the new block absent** settled it: **2 failures in 6** (runs 3 and 6),
against 3 in 10 with the block present. Indistinguishable. **The change was innocent, and the flake is
pre-existing.** The abort also lands at a different test file each time — `reformSkeletons`,
`findingService`, `financial` — so it is not one bad test but the engine being initialised or torn down
in workers that mock the database and never use it.

Registered as **MON-159** (medium), with the measurement, the exact panic signature, and an explicit
statement of what has *not* been investigated. Its `rootCause` is deliberately **empty**, following the
MON-147 precedent: the panic is inside Prisma's native engine, no line in this codebase is verified to
cause it, and inventing an anchor is the §19.2 failure the four-step audit exists to prevent.

The lesson is the reason it is filed rather than shrugged at: an intermittent red build that lands
somewhere different each time is the ideal environment for a wrong conclusion — either a session rewrites
working code, or it learns to re-run until green and stops reading failures at all. The second is how a
genuine regression ships.
